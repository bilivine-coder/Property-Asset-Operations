What it costs if you don't act

These aren't hypotheticals. They're the real risks that multiply with every lease cycle.

Automatic renewal you didn't want

Leases across the Kingdom renew automatically unless either party gives notice at least 60 days before expiry. Leases with 90 days or less remaining at enactment, and contracts of three months or less, follow their own terms. Miss the window and you've committed to another term.

Locked in

A lease with little legal effect

A lease not registered on Ejar carries little or no judicial or administrative effect — though arbitral claims have still been accepted on them. The contract exists; the protection is thinner than it looks.

Little legal effect

A site that can't be licensed

The building permit is a required attachment for a commercial licence, and the requirement varies by activity. A mismatch surfaces at licensing, not at signing.

Operation blocked

A registration window missed

Title registration is rolling out district by district, with penalties reported at up to SAR 100,000 for owners who miss their window. The date depends on which district the property falls in.

Penalty risk

A cost with no named owner

Electrical upgrades, water connections, fines, restoration at exit. Every element not assigned contractually becomes the argument later.

Margin erosion

A portfolio with no single view

Dates in PDFs, trackers in Excel, obligations in people's memory. The information exists — it just doesn't live in one place, and no single view shows what's exposed.

Invisible risk

The manual grind that never shows up on a scorecard

Three layers of detail quietly eat the week — and none of them are "managing the asset."

Critical dates, scattered across documents

Every lease carries renewal dates, escalation steps, deposit terms and notice windows. Across a client portfolio, those live in signed PDFs, Excel tabs and memory — and a spreadsheet cannot warn anyone that a date is approaching.

Site readiness, checked portal by portal

Permit, title deed, plans against reality, registry status, meters, electrical load, parking. Each sits in a different government system, and the approvals required change with the activity.

Clauses reviewed from scratch, every time

Who pays for the load increase. Who carries the fines. Who restores the unit at exit. Reviewed by memory, on documents where the answer is buried on page nine.

How automation cuts the load

Three components, each aimed at the biggest piece of the grind above. Nothing is approved automatically — the system assembles the picture and leaves the judgement with you.

Lease intelligence

Contracts & Ejar numbers dates & 60-day rule checked 90/60/30 alerts

Arabic and English contracts are read, dates and obligations extracted, and a renewal calendar built with alerts and Ejar flags.

Ejar flags work from the contract numbers and documents you supply — Ejar publishes no developer lookup, so nothing is scraped or looked up automatically.

Site readiness check

Permit, deed, plans, meters activity-specific requirements gap report per site

A checklist per activity type, with document collection and a gap report for each site before it opens.

Clause assignment

Draft & signed leases checked against who-pays library unassigned costs flagged

A clause library plus a reviewer that flags any cost with no named owner in the agreement.
